I'm Dharmjeet Singh — an independent Android developer from Bihar, India, building under the SynFusion name.

My work sits between practical engineering and interface design. Most of it is native Android: Kotlin, Jetpack Compose and Material 3, with Room and DataStore for data that has to survive a dead network. I like the constraints of the platform — small screens, limited memory, interrupted attention — because they force clarity.

WorkDaily began with a simple observation: daily-wage workers often keep attendance and earnings in a notebook. The app keeps that record offline, handles overtime and advances, and exports a PDF passbook. Office Mitra applies the same thinking to attendance, leave, approvals and salary workflows. TurtleX asks what a focused coding workspace could feel like on a phone, and Jump Adventure is a small platformer built to learn rendering and game loops properly.

I'm early in this journey, and I'm comfortable saying that. Every project adds a pattern, a technique or a better question — and the next one gets a little more considered. The long-term goal is straightforward: build useful software products, keep them honest, and keep learning in public.
